Key Summary

Based on the discussion today, IMG would like Purple Sand to present two options/paths for the project — set out side by side below, with the cost and commercial case behind each.

1. Project Overview and Recommendation

⚠ The basement is the single biggest cost risk on this project

Hickory's initial construction quote of $153M is closely aligned with Purple Sand's own estimate of $161.4M — a good sign the broader cost base is well understood. However, Hickory has quoted the basement alone at $30–35M for just one level of basement carpark. Because the site sits in a flood zone, IMG's own expectation is that Hickory's final number will climb well beyond the initial quote — potentially toward $200M once flood mitigation, waterproofing and retention costs are fully priced. That is a further increase of roughly $47–50M above the original $153M figure, and the basement is the primary driver of it.

OPTION 1 — Build Now, on the Current Endorsed Plan
  • Locks in the basement, and its full flood-zone cost risk
  • Quote may still move from $153M toward ~$200M before it's finalised
  • No opportunity to test podium parking or the efficiency upside
  • Certainty of starting sooner — but on an unresolved cost base
OPTION 2 — 3-Month Purple Sand Review First
  • Tests whether the basement can be removed or reduced, with parking moved to the podium
  • Avoids some or all of the $30–35M+ basement cost (potentially more, given flood-zone risk)
  • Also tests an NSA efficiency uplift of 8–12%, worth $19M–$30M in additional profit
  • Costs approximately $1.5M in fees and holding costs over the review period

4. The Decision

IMG can either take Option 1 — build now on the current endorsed plan, locking in the basement and its unresolved flood-zone cost risk on a Hickory quote that may still move from $153M toward $200M — or take Option 2 — spend approximately $1.5M and 3 months with Purple Sand to properly test removing the basement, moving parking into the podium, and lifting NSA efficiency. On the numbers above, Option 2 is net-positive by $47M to $64M more than Option 1's own outcome, before allowing for any further basement cost escalation that Option 1 would fully absorb.
